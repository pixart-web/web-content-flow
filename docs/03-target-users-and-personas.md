# Target Users and Personas

## Segmentation recommendation

The initial audience includes freelancers, small agencies, entrepreneurs, SMBs, e-commerce brands, creators, and startup marketing teams. For MVP research and acquisition, prioritize users who personally create content, manage multiple output types, and can decide to buy without procurement.

The first paying persona remains unresolved; see [Open Decisions](13-open-decisions.md).

## Persona 1 — Freelance marketer

**Context:** Manages strategy and content for several small clients, often working alone and switching between brands.

- **Objectives:** onboard clients faster; produce coherent deliverables; reuse repeatable processes; protect margin.
- **Frustrations:** incomplete briefs, repeated prompting, scattered files, revision loops, and inconsistent client voice.
- **Existing tools:** forms, documents, spreadsheets, chatbots, design tools, cloud storage, and social schedulers.
- **Buying triggers:** a new client, a fixed-price content package, growing workload, or pressure to shorten turnaround.
- **Objections:** output may feel generic; client data may be exposed; another workspace may add overhead; exports may be inadequate.
- **Most valuable outcomes:** reusable Brand Profile, multi-channel starter pack, item-level regeneration, organized export.
- **Likely plan / sensitivity:** individual or professional plan; price-sensitive but willing to pay when saved production time is visible and client workspaces are clearly separated.

## Persona 2 — Small agency owner

**Context:** Leads a small team delivering brand, website, campaign, or recurring content work. Processes exist but vary by employee and client.

- **Objectives:** standardize discovery and first drafts; increase throughput; maintain quality; make delegation easier.
- **Frustrations:** inconsistent strategist output, context lost in handoffs, duplicated research, and hard-to-review AI use.
- **Existing tools:** project management, shared drives, documents, chatbots, design tools, website builders, and schedulers.
- **Buying triggers:** team growth, margin pressure, several simultaneous launches, or a need for standardized AI governance.
- **Objections:** MVP collaboration is too limited; team permissions are absent; output quality may not meet agency standards; client export may lack branding.
- **Most valuable outcomes:** repeatable project workflow, shared brand context, consistent module output, version history, packaged exports.
- **Likely plan / sensitivity:** team plan later; less price-sensitive than individuals but expects seats, permissions, and predictable usage before broader adoption. MVP may initially serve one operator per account.

## Persona 3 — Entrepreneur

**Context:** Is launching or repositioning a small business and must make marketing decisions without a dedicated marketing team.

- **Objectives:** clarify the offer; create a credible brand foundation; launch a website and initial campaign quickly.
- **Frustrations:** does not know what to create, receives inconsistent AI answers, and struggles to judge marketing quality.
- **Existing tools:** general chatbots, website builders, templates, design tools, notes, and occasional freelancers.
- **Buying triggers:** imminent launch, a new offer, limited contractor budget, or dissatisfaction with a fragmented DIY process.
- **Objections:** guided brief may feel long; recommendations may appear arbitrary; generated work still requires editing; recurring value after launch is unclear.
- **Most valuable outcomes:** guided decisions, clear Brand Profile, website structure and copy, launch content pack, practical calls to action.
- **Likely plan / sensitivity:** starter plan, project pass, or trial; high price sensitivity and possible episodic usage.

## Persona 4 — E-commerce marketing manager

**Context:** Owns ongoing campaigns and product content for a small or mid-sized online store, with limited internal creative capacity.

- **Objectives:** accelerate campaign planning; adapt one offer across channels; keep product and brand messages consistent.
- **Frustrations:** high content volume, repetitive adaptation, inconsistent claims, seasonal deadlines, and asset retrieval.
- **Existing tools:** commerce platform, product information, email platform, spreadsheets, design tools, chatbots, and advertising managers.
- **Buying triggers:** a product launch, seasonal campaign, catalogue expansion, or rising content-production cost.
- **Objections:** missing commerce integrations, factual hallucinations, weak product-level workflows, and no direct publishing.
- **Most valuable outcomes:** campaign-aligned advertisements, social posts, scripts, product descriptions, calendar, and reusable creative direction.
- **Likely plan / sensitivity:** professional or usage-based plan; values predictable generation allowance and may pay more for product import and integrations after MVP.

## Cross-persona needs

- A short path to first useful output.
- Clear distinction between supplied facts and generated recommendations.
- Reliable project isolation and data handling.
- Content that can be edited at item level.
- Consistency without repetitive configuration.
- Transparent generation state and recoverable errors.
- Exports that fit existing production tools.

## MVP research priority

**Recommendation:** Interview and test freelancers and entrepreneurs first because they combine direct buying authority, hands-on usage, and low collaboration requirements. Include small agency owners to test expansion value and e-commerce managers to test recurring content demand. Do not finalize positioning or pricing until behavior and willingness-to-pay evidence is collected.
