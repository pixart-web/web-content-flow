# Marketing Website Direction

## Objective and target emotion

Explain the connected-workflow advantage, demonstrate credible product structure, and move qualified visitors to create an account or join a clearly labelled validation list. Visitors should feel clarity, confidence, and relief—not spectacle.

All positioning copy and pricing are working hypotheses. Do not invent customer proof.

## Navigation

Logo; Product; How it works; Use cases; Modules; Pricing placeholder when applicable; Sign in; `Create account` primary action. A single-page anchored MVP site is sufficient; no marketing CMS is required.

## Page structure

### 1. Hero

- Working headline: **Build Your Entire Brand From One Brief.**
- Support: **Create your identity, website, social content, campaigns and creative direction inside one connected workflow.**
- Primary CTA: `Create your first project` or `Create account`, depending on availability.
- Secondary CTA: `See how it works`.
- Add a concise boundary/proof line describing editable Brand Profile and content modules.
- Visually show one brief entering an ordered flow and a connected output system emerging.

### 2. Animated product flow

Use a straight-on product concept composed of a brief card, Brand Profile hub, and aligned module cards. Suggested truthful progression:

```text
Brief received
Brand Profile created
Website generated
Social campaign created
Ad concepts ready
Video scripts ready
```

The sequence uses labelled status, calm connecting lines, and reduced-motion static state. Mark as conceptual until it reflects implemented UI.

### 3. Social proof

Before verified proof exists, use a labelled area such as `Customer evidence will be added after the validation program.` Prefer product-method proof—structured outputs, versioning, shared context—over fake logos or numeric claims. Remove the section if the placeholder reduces credibility.

### 4. Outcome cards

Three or four cards: `Launch a brand`, `Build a website`, `Create a campaign`, `Plan a month of content`. Each names inputs, outputs, and editing/export control. Avoid feature-count grids.

### 5. Workflow section

Four steps: Describe the project → Review Brand Profile → Generate selected modules → Edit and export. Show connection and user control; do not imply automatic publishing.

### 6. Comparison

Compare workflow properties, not unverifiable superiority:

| Fragmented workflow | Web Content Flow intended workflow |
|---|---|
| Context copied into separate tools | One Brand Profile shared by selected modules |
| Outputs stored in chats/documents | Structured project content items |
| Whole response regenerated | One item regenerated with prior version retained |
| Status hidden in separate tabs | Project-level generation status |

Label as a product-design comparison, not measured customer results.

### 7. Product previews

Show Brand Profile, website page structure, social card, ad concept, and job status using realistic demo content. Each preview has one explanatory caption and no out-of-scope controls.

### 8. Use cases

Initially address freelance marketers, entrepreneurs, small agencies, and e-commerce marketers. Keep benefits specific and label persona priority as under validation.

### 9. Testimonials

Do not publish fabricated quotes, names, portraits, or logos. Use this section only after consented research or customer evidence exists; until then, omit it or label the planned evidence format internally.

### 10. Pricing placeholder

Show `Pricing is being validated` or a waitlist/pilot invitation only if commercially appropriate. Do not invent plan benefits or crossed-out prices. Link to the unresolved pricing decision.

### 11. FAQ

Answer: what the product creates; how consistency works; whether content is editable; mock vs real mode if public; what it does not replace; data/provider handling once decided; publishing boundary; supported language caveat.

### 12. Final CTA and footer

Restate one-brief outcome with one primary action. Footer includes Product, Use cases, Help, Privacy, Terms, and account links only when destinations exist.

## Visual direction

- Light canvas, Dark Navy text, focused brand-gradient hero connector.
- 1120–1280 px content width, 80–128 px section spacing.
- Real product previews dominate imagery.
- Poppins for hero/major headings; Inter for proof and explanations.
- No excessive parallax, glass cards, or continuous background animation.
