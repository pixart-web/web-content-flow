# Illustration and Imagery

## Priority order

1. Realistic product UI previews built from the actual or explicitly conceptual interface.
2. Structured product diagrams showing one input becoming connected outputs.
3. Relevant, licensed photography where it adds human/business context.
4. Restrained abstract graphics for atmosphere.

Generic AI artwork is not a substitute for product evidence.

## Marketing imagery

- Show the workflow: brief summary → Brand Profile → aligned module outputs.
- Use clean perspective or shallow depth only when text remains legible; straight-on views are preferred for proof.
- Label conceptual previews as “Product concept” until implemented.
- Use brand gradient as a connector or background wash, not a full-screen neon field.

## Dashboard and screenshot treatment

- Use realistic content lengths, job states, dates, and module labels.
- Never show invented analytics, customers, integrations, or publishing controls.
- Frame screenshots with 1 px neutral border, 12–16 px radius, and restrained shadow.
- Crop to the feature being explained; preserve enough shell to maintain orientation.
- Hide or replace personal and sensitive information with clearly fictional demo content.

## Concept-card imagery

Creative-concept cards may show a restrained thumbnail area for a visual direction or image prompt. Use consistent aspect ratios (4:3 default, 1:1 when channel-relevant), neutral placeholders, and explicit “Concept” or “Prompt” labels. Do not imply image rendering is an MVP capability.

## Photography

- Prefer authentic work contexts, products, materials, and small teams in natural light.
- Favor editorial composition, neutral environments, and diverse representation without tokenistic staging.
- Avoid people staring at holograms, exaggerated excitement, corporate handshakes, and generic laptop stock imagery.
- Confirm licensing, releases, cropping rights, and regional appropriateness.

## Abstract graphics and texture

- Use ordered blocks, aligned nodes, layered documents, subtle grid lines, and controlled branching.
- Background texture may use 1–2% neutral noise or a 24–32 px grid at very low contrast.
- Gradient mesh is marketing-only: two or three broad fields, 8–18% opacity on light backgrounds, no sharp neon blooms.
- Do not place texture behind dense content or form controls.

## Image-generation prompt style

When generating concept imagery, prompts should specify:

```text
subject and business context; intended channel; composition and crop;
lighting; material/color direction; realistic production style;
brand palette relationship; negative constraints; space for copy if required
```

Example: “Editorial product still life for a premium sustainable skincare launch, 4:5 portrait, soft directional daylight, limestone and translucent materials, restrained indigo accent in packaging details, ample negative space upper left for headline, realistic photography, no text, no neon, no floating UI, no AI symbols.”

Generated concepts require human review for rights, brand fit, factual representation, and platform policy.

## Prohibited imagery

- Robots, brains, wands, sparkles, glowing orbs, circuit faces, chat bubbles as hero concept.
- Chaotic node networks or branching lines without clear hierarchy.
- Fake customer logos, testimonials, user counts, or screenshots.
- Screens showing out-of-scope video rendering, website publishing, social publishing, or advanced analytics as MVP.
- Excessive glass, iridescence, chrome 3D, or cyberpunk gradients.
